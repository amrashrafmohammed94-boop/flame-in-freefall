    About Flame in Freefall

    A fire-risk tool for crews in spacecraft, Moon and Mars habitats, built on NASA microgravity combustion data for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.

    The problem

    Future Moon bases are expected to run at about 8.2 psi and 34% oxygen, more flammable than the ISS. NASA has decades of fire tests in space, but the results sit in many separate tables, and no public test has burned a real material in Moon-base air.

    What the tool does

    1. Where are you? Choose a habitat: pressure, oxygen, nitrogen and gravity appear automatically.
    2. What could burn? Type a material: its family and usual state appear; you confirm solid, liquid or gas.
    3. Assessment: chance the fire goes out by itself, how long it can burn, a Low / Medium / High risk, how well NASA data covers the case, and a Warn–Gather–Work response with the right extinguishing agent.
    4. Explore: compare one material across all habitats, move an oxygen slider, and print an emergency card.

    Data

    All 20 public experiment tables from 18 ISS combustion investigations in NASA Physical Science Informatics (PSI), downloaded 14 Nov 2026 (CC0). Main sources: FLEX (PSI-69, DOI 10.60555/mbq8-0451), ACME CFI-G (PSI-159, DOI 10.60555/cyt1-kh64), Saffire-I/II (PSI-98/99), BASS-II (PSI-25), SAME/SAME-R (PSI-102/101). Habitat atmospheres: NASA "Moon to Mars: Exploration Atmosphere" (2024) and NASA decompression-sickness plan (2022). Fire response: NASA "Emergency Operations History" (SpaceOps 2023).

    How the prediction works (AI / machine learning)

    • Liquids: k-nearest-neighbours over 273 FLEX tests: the 15 tests closest in oxygen and pressure (same fuel class) give the chance of self-extinction and the burn time. A logistic-regression model on the same data reaches a cross-validated ROC-AUC of 0.85.
    • Gases: CFI-G gas-flame results by oxygen level and pressure band.
    • Solids: material class from Saffire, BASS and SAME (spreads, slow, no spread, smoke) – an estimate, because NASA has no solid-material data at Moon-base air.
    • Risk: ≥70% chance of self-extinction = Low, 30–70% = Medium, <30% = High; one level higher when the case is outside NASA data or in partial gravity; pure oxygen and lithium batteries = High.

    Limits

    • Droplet and burner flames show the trend, not the size of a real cabin fire.
    • No partial-gravity or spacesuit-condition fire data exists yet (LUCI and FM2 are planned).
    • Rover and Mars atmospheres are assumed equal to the Moon base. Screening tool, not a certified procedure.

    Use of AI in building this project

    The team used Claude (Anthropic) as an assistant to download and analyse the NASA tables, draft the risk logic and write the website code. All findings were checked against the original NASA data, and the risk limits and safety actions were reviewed by the team's fire-safety engineer.
